Cartesian Plane & Graphs
theory

The Cartesian plane is determined by two perpendicular axes: the horizontal x-axis and the vertical y-axis.

“The graph of an equation is the curve whose points satisfy the equation.”

Given an equation y = f(x), its graph is the set of all points (x, f(x)) in the plane.

Examples:

  • y = ax + b – straight line
  • y = x² – parabola
  • y = 1/x – hyperbola (two branches)
y = 2x + 1      // line with slope 2, intercept 1
y = x*x         // parabola opening upwards
y = 1/x         // hyperbola, undefined at x = 0
Straight Lines & Slope
theory

A straight line can be written as y = mx + c, where:

  • m – slope (rate of change)
  • c – y-intercept (value at x = 0)

Given two points (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂), the slope is

m = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁)

The equation of the line through these points is

y = y₁ + (y₂ − y₁) * (x − x₁) / (x₂ − x₁)
Transformations of Graphs
theory

Starting from a base function y = f(x), we can transform its graph:

  • y = f(x) + c – vertical shift
  • y = a f(x) – vertical scaling
  • y = f(x - h) – horizontal shift
  • y = f(-x) – reflection in the y-axis

We will visualize these transformations interactively on the right.
